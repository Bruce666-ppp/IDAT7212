KneeUp is a wearable prototype combining sensing with pneumatic support: it senses movement, interprets the user's state, calculates assistance and inflates air cells. Explore the 3D laboratory, apps, live demonstrations, presentation and videos here. Sensor data in the web demonstration is simulated; real-time Arduino integration has not been completed.

The annual incidence of meniscal injury is approximately 61 per 100,000 people, making it a common concern in sports medicine.

Who it is designed for

Older adults who remain independently mobile and want to keep walking the paths they choose. Everyday mobility is the starting point, professional follow-up is an extension, and hiking is an activity they hope to retain.

Positioning: a mobility-assistance prototype for adults aged 60–75

3D Laboratory · Four modes

The original 128,000-face CAD model was compressed from a 196 MB OBJ to a 4.4 MB Draco GLB. Three.js renders it locally, with interaction available offline after the files load. Drag to rotate, scroll to zoom and use the four modes below:

1Product detail Slow automatic rotation 2Wearing relationship Ghost leg with a translucent brace 3Internal structure Femur / tibia / patella / meniscus 4Regional heat map Seven self-report hotspots

Live · On-device inference

Live pose demo: knee motion measured in the browser

Camera frames are processed inside your browser; no video is uploaded. Left and right knee angles, asymmetry and squat counts update in real time. You can also upload a squat video instead of using a camera. Channel B is a simulated textile-strain channel.
